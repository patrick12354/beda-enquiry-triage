Twelve items

Twelve messages, three documents, five CRM rows and four people. Some of it is the same customer twice. Some of it contradicts itself. One item is a robot and one is a fraud. This is a system that works out which is which, checks the numbers itself, and then refuses to send a single reply without a person.

Chapter I

Four shapes, one queue

The supplied pack arrives as four different things: twelve emails, three document extracts, a five-row CRM export, and a staff directory naming four people and what each of them owns.

Only the directory and the CRM have structure. Everything else is prose written by strangers who had no reason to be tidy, and two of the twelve were not written by a person at all.

So the first job is not intelligence. It is turning four shapes into one record — which is ordinary code, and which is where most of the reliability in this system actually comes from. Attachments are kept beside their message rather than glued into it, because later the system has to be able to say which document a number was read from.

Who owns what, according to the directory

  • Matt CooperFounder. Major commercial opportunities and strategic partnerships.
  • Ties RahardjoExecutive Operations Coordinator. Scheduling, administration, logistics, general operational enquiries.
  • Zidane MouldinoMarketing and Growth Coordinator. Marketing, website and inbound growth enquiries.
  • Ali PratamaSenior Business Analyst. CRM, systems, data, workflows and infrastructure.
  • No engineerThere is nobody in this directory who can answer E006. Chapter VI is about what the system does with that.

Every routing decision in the build quotes the line above that justifies it. Ownership is never inferred from topic alone.

Chapter II

Three kinds of duplicate, three different answers

Every brief about duplicates assumes there is one problem called "duplicates". This pack contains three, and treating them the same way gets two of them wrong.

One · inside the CRM

C001 and C002

Same contact, same city, same email domain, and a company name that differs only by a legal suffix and a plural. One customer, recorded twice, sitting at two different pipeline stages — so BEDA's pipeline report is double-counting them today.

No email created this and no email fixes it. The system scans the export against itself and raises MERGE-001 — proposed, not applied. Choosing a survivor is a business decision, and un-merging is manual.

Two · a second bite

E001 and E002

Amelia Grant writes from her own address with a bill attached, then the same enquiry arrives through the website form from a second address, with the company name spelled differently and the consumption stated more vaguely.

Linked by email domain. E001 is filed and answered; E002 is logged, not answered. Two replies about one warehouse portfolio is how a customer learns nobody is reading.

Three · not a duplicate at all

E009 and E010

Sam gives a mobile number, then writes again: "Just correcting my number… It is 0411 999 102, not 0411 999 120." Two digits transposed. Treated as a duplicate, the system would keep the wrong number and answer twice.

Recognised as an amendment. The correction supersedes, the superseded value stays in the audit log, the record already staged is amended in the same run, and no second reply is drafted.

Only one rule can resolve a conflict automatically: a later item that says it is a correction. Two values that merely differ, with nobody stating which is right, is an open question — so the system holds both, blocks anything that would act on either, and says so. E001 and E002 disagree about five fields that way, including whether the company is "Hume Logistics Pty Ltd" or "Hume Logistic".

There is no model in any of this. Asking an LLM "are these the same company?" is non-reproducible, unauditable, and worse at the job than normalisation and a similarity score you can read.

Chapter III

Subtraction should never be probabilistic

E003 claims invoice 1847 is $2,640 above the purchase order. The attached document holds both numbers. A model asked to check this will usually get it right and will occasionally, quietly, get it wrong — and the output is heading for a finance conversation.

So the check is code. It reads the two figures out of the document, subtracts, compares against the claim, and returns one of three verdicts. "I could not check this" is a real answer, and collapsing it into "agrees" is exactly how an unverified number acquires the appearance of verification.

The first version of this check reported a variance of $41,119 and announced that the customer was wrong. It had read Purchase order: GF PO 8821 and taken the reference number as a dollar value. Every part of that output looked like arithmetic. The fix is one line — a money figure must carry a currency symbol — and the reason it is worth telling is that this is the failure mode the whole design is built around: not a model that hallucinates obviously, but a plausible number nobody questions.

The same module handles the case that is not a discrepancy. E001 says about 2.1 GWh a year across three warehouses; the attached bill is 68,420 kWh for one site, for July. Those are both true and they are not the same fact.

A system that files 68,420 kWh as this customer's consumption has lost an order of magnitude of opportunity. So the check resolves nothing: it states the scope mismatch, does the extrapolation once and labels it arithmetic, not a measurement, and asks for the other two bills before anything is sized.

Chapter IV

There are five ways out, and one of them is silence

Every item leaves the pipeline through exactly one of these. The action is chosen by deterministic code — never by a model — because who gets called back is business policy, and policy belongs somewhere a person can diff it.

Means
No record, no reply, retained for appeal. Never written to a system of record.
In the pack
E004
Note
Stopped before inference. Junk is the most likely carrier of a prompt injection, and the cheapest defence is not feeding it to a model.

The floor

Being wrong about spam is the expensive one

Why
A misrouted lead is noticed. A real customer silently binned is not — nobody reports the enquiry they never knew arrived.
So
The spam confidence floor is 0.95. Anything below it goes to a person, not to the bin.
And
Quarantine is retained and reversible, which is the only reason it is allowed to be automatic at all.

      Chapter VI

      What it will not do

      Eight replies were written. None of them can be sent, and not because a setting says so.

      The drafting module returns a string. It has no transport, no address book and no send function, and neither does anything that calls it. The approval queue can mark a draft cleared by a named person; releasing it belongs to an outbound service this build does not contain and holds no reference to.

      Stated plainly: if every model in the system were fully compromised — classifier, extractor and drafter all returning attacker-chosen output — zero emails are sent. The worst available outcome is a wrong label, which the deterministic gate still has to accept, on a draft a human still has to approve.

      The same instinct decided what happens to the CRM. E011 says the sync has been failing since 02:14 with 146 records unsynchronised. Writing into a system known to be inconsistent turns a sync failure into a data-loss incident, so the export is read-only: the pipeline stages its own records, proposes merges, and applies nothing. Every item processed after that alert carries a warning that its CRM match was made against data of unknown freshness.

      And E006 asks for an engineer to confirm THD limits at the point of common coupling on a 500 kW battery. There is no engineer in the directory. Any of the four names could have been made to sound plausible. The system names the gap instead, drafts an acknowledgement that deliberately does not answer the question, and asks Matt to nominate someone qualified.
